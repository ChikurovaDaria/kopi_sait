/ apples

An apple is just the beginning The taste is the whole story

FOR THE FIRST TWO YEARS WE BARELY SOLD ANYTHING — WE JUST WATCHED HOW EACH VARIETY BEHAVED ON THIS HILL, IN THIS FOG, IN THIS COLD. SOME DIDN'T MAKE IT. THE ONES THAT STAYED DEVELOPED SOMETHING YOU CAN ACTUALLY TASTE. BELOW IS HOW WE READ THEM

Gravenhill

Named after the old estate. The only variety that was already here when the brothers arrived. Dark olive-green, weathered. Asymmetrical, slightly lopsided. The oldest-looking apple you'll ever hold. Skin is rough, almost leathery.

Sundrop

The only variety that faces south. Gets more light than anything else on the plot. Golden yellow, almost glowing when the light hits it right. Perfectly round, smooth skin. The prettiest apple on the hill — and it knows it.

Coldspan

First frost hits the hill harder than the valley. This variety was bred for exactly that. Bright scarlet with sharp white lenticels. Small, almost aggressive in shape — like it's bracing for something. Skin is thick and waxy.

HALFTIDE

Ripens later than everything else on the hill. Always the last one standing. Warm amber-yellow, streaked with faint russet. Matte skin, almost dusty to the touch. Irregular shape — no two look alike.

FOGBORN

Named after the morning fog that sits on the hill longer than anywhere else. Pale green with a faint blush. Thin skin, almost translucent near the stem. Bruises easily — handle with care.

RONA

Deep burgundy, almost bruised-looking skin. Surprisingly heavy for its size. Flesh is dense and ivory-white, slow to oxidize.
